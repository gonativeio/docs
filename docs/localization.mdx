Your app shows content in two layers, and each layer is localized separately:

- **Web content** is your website, displayed in the app. Your website controls its own translations, just as it does in a browser.
- **Native interface** is everything Median renders outside your website, such as dialog buttons and the menu that appears when a user long-presses a link. Median translates these strings for the languages you enable in the App Studio.

## Web content

Your app passes the user's device language to your website in the `Accept-Language` header, the same way a mobile browser does. If your website already serves translated content based on that header, it works in the app with no changes. This works for any language your website supports.

<Tip>
  You can also read the user's language in JavaScript with `navigator.language`.
  This is useful if your site switches languages on the client side instead of
  the server.
</Tip>

## Native interface

Median translates these native strings:

- **Dialog buttons:** OK, Cancel, Done, View, and Menu
- **Link menu** (shown when a user long-presses a link): Open link in external browser, Copy link address, and Share link

### Enable languages

1. In the App Studio, go to **Interface → Localization**.
2. Select the languages you want to include.
3. Rebuild your app. Language changes only take effect in a new build.

If a user's device language isn't enabled in your app, the native strings are shown in English.

## Request a language or report a translation

If your language isn't listed, or a translation is missing or incorrect, [contact our team](https://median.co/contact) with the language and the strings you'd like added or changed. We'll update the translations and announce the change in the [Release Notes](/docs/release-notes).

<Note>
  Your App Store and Google Play listings are localized separately in App Store
  Connect and Google Play Console.
</Note>
